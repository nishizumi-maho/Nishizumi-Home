The Nishizumi Style

This hangar takes its name and bearing from Maho Nishizumi — the composed, exacting commander of Kuromorimine Girls Academy in Girls und Panzer. The tools here are built the way she leads a Panzer line: disciplined, deliberate, and pointed squarely at the objective. No wasted motion, no guesswork left on the table — just clean instrumentation for drivers who take the sim seriously.

Nishizumi-SR

Stable release

A live Safety Rating estimator — a transparent desktop overlay plus a companion web calculator that share the exact same formula. It shows your Estimated SR gain/loss during a session, built on a CPI→SR mapping reconstructed from iRacing's own official data (MAE ~0.007 SR).

Hardware & VR

Bridging iRacing to the metal — force-feedback bases and PlayStation VR2.

iRacing MOZA

Nishizumi-PitLink

Stable release

A Windows tray app that bridges iRacing's detected car (and optionally track) to the matching MOZA Pit House Motor preset. It's a connector, not an FFB editor — presets are read fresh from Pit House's own folder and pushed through the official MOZA SDK, so Pit House stays the single source of truth.

View on GitHub
iRacing PSVR2

Nishizumi-PSVR2-iRacing-Haptics

Stable release

A Windows companion app that turns iRacing telemetry into PlayStation VR2 headset rumble through the PSVR2 Toolkit C API. Fully independent — it never bundles, patches, or jailbreaks the Toolkit — with comfort calibration and self-contained builds.

View on GitHub
PSVR2

Nishizumi-PSVR2-Ignition

Stable release

A Windows tray app that watches for your PSVR2 headset to wake over USB, then auto-runs vr2jb and launches SteamVR — no manual click. It keeps both vr2jb and itself updated in the background, so putting the headset on is all it takes.
